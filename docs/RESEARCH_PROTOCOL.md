# Do shared non-task breaks change the quality of subsequent team decisions?

## Proposed paper and closest precedent

Four stateless language-model sessions work through purchasing decisions under a fixed number of work turns. The intervention changes what happens during their break and what each session can recall afterward. The primary question is whether a shared non-task exchange changes the team's post-break decision yield relative to four private writing turns.

The hidden-profile paradigm has already been tested with LLMs. Toxtli and Delaflor varied synchronous/asynchronous communication and shared/hidden information in teams of three, then scored candidate selection. They found that surfacing evidence and integrating it into the decision can come apart. This rules out a credible claim to have invented hidden-profile LLM evaluation. Our proposed contribution is an intervention **outside the task channel**, a private-writing control with matched call opportunities, selective removal of break memory, and independently re-scorable work outcomes with costs. This targeted review establishes a plausible research gap. A systematic priority claim would require a broader search before submission. [CSCW Companion paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3785651.3831525)

## Work sample

Use supplier selection for the primary paper. It is a realistic *decision structure* with synthetic inputs. Field procurement involves policy, negotiation, uncertain utility and accountability. Removing these features lets us grade every choice by a declared rule. Real company outcomes would introduce incomparable cases and ambiguous ground truth. Keep the original task board as a regression fixture. For external validity, follow up with de-identified historical purchasing or planning cases that an independent domain expert has graded in advance. A separate real-repository maintenance study could use executable hidden tests, but would answer a different domain question.

Each case has four suppliers. The coordinator sees delivery reliability, the builder sees total cost value, the verifier sees quality assurance, and the integrator sees service coverage. Scores are integers from 1 to 9. Within each scorecard there are no ties. A generator accepts only cases with a unique joint optimum at least two points above the runner-up, and no individual's local favorite is the group winner. The four criteria carry equal weight by construction. The reference values are unavailable to provider prompts except for the role's own evidence or a score deliberately shared at work.

One turn can reveal up to two previously private scores, request a specific colleague's evidence, wait, or award a contract if the actor is the integrator. Each award is irreversible. Agents receive a practice case before the first break. Its result is excluded. Six fresh scored cases then become available in order, with a single work-turn budget to divide among them. A decision made with incomplete evidence is permitted. This introduces a real trade-off between coverage and decision quality. The evaluator compares scores directly without asking another LLM for a judgment.

## Conditions, mechanism and unit

| Arm | Break | What can enter later work context in the primary study |
|---|---|---|
| A | Silent rest | No break transcript |
| B | One private non-task writing call per agent | That agent's own private lines |
| C | One bounded interpersonal turn per agent | The department's exchanged lines with speaker attribution |

B and C share a topic, move schedule, number of choices per turn, output ceiling, and number of scheduled calls. Their actual input lengths, outputs, cache hits and retry counts can differ. Log those differences. C versus B estimates the **package** of interpersonal exchange and access to peers' lines. It cannot isolate reciprocity from the amount and wording of text seen. C versus A is the practical value of adding the whole break. B versus A helps interpret extra generation and private context.

Run a separate, predetermined **withheld-memory** cohort using the same case seeds as the attributed cohort. Breaks still occur and consume calls. Their text is omitted from every later work prompt. Under the stateless provider design, identical pre-break work states should then yield identical *wire inputs* across arms at the same role-turn. Stochastic outputs may still diverge. An **anonymous-memory** cohort keeps the words but replaces speaker labels. For a memory interaction, pair the two C−B differences by seed across cohorts; report that difference and a paired uncertainty interval. The built-in registry reports each cohort separately. The cross-cohort interaction is a planned secondary analysis. A stronger future control would replay an unrelated team's transcript with the same length, wording distribution and speaker labels.

One experimental unit is one **matched block**: three independently stored four-agent departments receive identical seed-specific cases, roles, model, work opportunities and evaluation. Within blocks, work call order rotates across roles from a seeded initial permutation. Cases, agents, and messages inside a block are repeated observations. Treating them as independent replicates would underestimate uncertainty. Separate model families and version epochs. Counterbalancing within blocks reduces clock-order bias but cannot eliminate provider-side drift or shared caches.

## Outcomes and analysis

For each arm, sum the points for the suppliers actually selected on the six scored cases. Divide by the total points attainable by choosing the best supplier on every one of those cases. An unawarded contract earns zero. An unstarted or interrupted **block** is missing and its allocation is retained.

For example, if six optimal awards would earn 150 points and a team earns 105, its decision yield is 70%. If B earns 65% on the same cases, this block contributes **+5 percentage points** to C−B. These are illustrative numbers. The registry averages one difference per completed block. It gives worst/best-case bounds across all allocated blocks by allowing each missing contrast to range from −100 to +100 pp. Its 95% descriptive interval resamples entire paired blocks using 5,000 draws and a reproducible seed. It is available for live cohorts with at least ten complete blocks. Ten is a display threshold, not a power calculation. Inspect the actual paired distribution and sensitivity to missingness.

| Outcome | Measure / denominator | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Primary productivity | Decision yield at the fixed work horizon | Combines quality and throughput and assigns zero to unfinished work |
| Quality versus speed | Optimal awards / six assigned, optimal awards / awards made, awards made / six | Detects a team that earns more by making more low-quality awards |
| Coordination | Distinct private score cells published, outside-role cells visible to the chair when awarding, requested scores and duplicate rejections in events | Availability and timing of evidence. Award accuracy measures integration separately |
| Resource efficiency | Work turns, break turns, attempts, tokens, cache hits, provider latency, estimated USD, uncertain reservations | Report incremental yield and incremental cost side by side. Cost per correct decision is undefined for a team with zero correct decisions |
| Fidelity / operational loss | Four intended social or writing turns per break, task-free accepted lines, invalid actions, provider or budget stops, model fingerprints | Distinguishes treatment exposure, model behavior and infrastructure loss |
| Reference baselines | Uniform supplier choice, integrator's local favorite, full-information optimum | These analytic reference policies assume all six awards are made. They do not consume the teams' observed communication budget |

Repeated disclosure is an action-level error. More messages alone do not imply more cooperative behavior. Task-board repair and versioned-handoff metrics belong to that environment. Avoid treating them as measurements of the purchasing task. The office poses are visual annotations of actions. They carry no scientific weight.

## Run the experiment

1. **Pilot on disposable seeds.** Verify that the practice case precedes the first break, all roles can use the interface, and the number of work rounds avoids floor and ceiling effects. Inspect a blind sample of chat exchanges for coherence, off-topic compliance and the limits of the dialogue templates. Set an acceptance rule for dialogue fidelity before drawing a confirmatory sample. Collect two independent ratings and their agreement; these ratings have not yet been collected.
2. **Choose a useful effect and budget.** For planning, a five-percentage-point absolute change might justify a break if its marginal cost is acceptable. Set that threshold with the intended user. If the pilot SD of paired differences were 0.15, a two-sided normal approximation at 5% and 80% power gives approximately `ceil((1.96 + 0.84)^2 × 0.15² / 0.05²) = 71` independent blocks. This is an example. Use pilot variability and likely missingness to simulate the actual interval/test before fixing N. Pilot seeds stay out of confirmation. Run separate powered cohorts for replication if making model-specific claims.
3. **Register before observing confirmation results.** In Studies, choose the model, criteria, days, rounds, memory policy, held-out case seeds, and per-block ceilings. Record the minimum meaningful difference, decision rule for missingness, stopping rule, planned contrasts, model/version policy and analysis in a timestamped external preregistration. The local hash makes changes detectable against an archived copy. It gives no independent timestamp or third-party attestation.
4. **Execute all assigned blocks.** Use Open block for a single block or the sequential CLI listed in the README. Keep the same verified build, role prompts and scoring rules. Freeze model variants separately. Record every failed, incomplete and capped block. Preserve deployment timestamps and provider-reported model fingerprints. Avoid stopping when the live chart looks favorable.
5. **Audit and export.** Export the evidence packet before analysis. Its independent scorer reads decision actions and reference scorecards, verifies event hashes, and regenerates a case-level CSV. Save the protocol hash and archive checksum outside the local database. Divide each outcome by its declared denominator. For the primary effect, graph paired block differences and show both uncertainty and attrition bounds. Prespecify whether secondary contrasts are descriptive or require multiple-testing adjustment.

## What a paper could conclude

A positive, replicated C−B difference with adequate precision would support the claim that this particular break-and-memory intervention improved decision yield for the tested models and case generator. A negative difference would quantify a performance cost. If the confidence interval includes materially positive and negative effects, the result is inconclusive. A tight interval inside a pre-specified practical-equivalence band can support a bounded negligible-effect claim under an appropriate equivalence analysis. A mechanism claim requires its own manipulated comparison; a correlation between disclosures and yield is insufficient.

**Paper outline.** Introduce the engineering choice of spending extra model calls on shared non-task context. Position the study against human workplace research, hidden-profile LLM experiments, and communication benchmarks. Describe the case generator, role projections, dialogue corpus, counterbalancing, version locks and scoring. Report the primary paired result first, then a quality/throughput decomposition, costs, fidelity failures, memory ablations and replication. Include a chart of one auditable decision showing what the chair knew when it awarded the contract. Release the frozen protocol, code, generated casebooks, budget assumptions, exclusions and replication packets. Discuss constrained English dialogue, synthetic cardinal utilities, fixed roles, the single integrator, provider drift and the absence of field validation.
